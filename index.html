Family 1 · vs 2-3 Zone

Our Set vs 2-3

One look, called on the way up: Base · Extend · Drag · Skip — the guard calls one word as he brings the ball up, so everyone knows the finish before it starts.

One look — every variation starts the same: swing it to a wing, 5 flashes to the elbow, 4 runs the baseline. The defense sees the same start every time — only the finish changes.

Passer rule — one rule for every call: The passer slides to the corner only when it's empty. If 4 is going out there (Extend, Drag), the passer holds at the wing and becomes the kick-out there.

Play 1 · Our Set · Call: BASE

Our Set — High-Low

The idea: This is our own set against a 2-3 — wings stretched wide, 5 in the middle of the zone, and 4 starting on the right low block. The ball swings right, 5 flashes and catches at the RIGHT elbow, and 4 waits on the LEFT block — opposite 5. Then the timing takes over: 5 turns and makes his move toward the basket, the zone's bottom defender on the RIGHT block steps UP to help on him — and on that step-up, not before, 4 cuts across the lane behind the defense to the now-vacated RIGHT block and 5 feeds him for the layup. Meanwhile 2, who fed 5, slides down to the right corner, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out — and as 2 goes, 1 slides over from the top to fill the right wing spot 2 just left, so 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side and the right-side spacing stays honest. 3 does not move — he stays home, wide on the left wing, and if the top of the zone collapses on 5 instead, he is open there for the skip-pass three.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

Call on the way up: BASE — one word from the guard, and everyone knows the finish. One look — every variation starts the same. This is Base: 4 waits opposite, then — the moment the right-block defender steps up to 5 — cuts behind him to the vacated right block for the feed. The corner is empty in Base, so the passer rule lets 2 slide down to the corner after he feeds 5 — 1 fills behind him at the right wing, and 3 stays home — he's the skip.

Same play, other direction — learn both.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up in our set: 1 top, 2 & 3 wide and deep, 5 high post, 4 on the RIGHT low block for this rep.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing.
  3. 5 flashes hard to the RIGHT elbow. 4 runs the baseline block to block behind the zone to the LEFT block — opposite 5 — and waits there. 3 stays home, wide on the left wing — he's the skip.
  4. 2 feeds 5 at the right elbow — get the ball into the middle of the zone. Right after the feed, 2 slides down to the right corner, feet set behind the three-point line, spotted up and ready. As 2 goes, 1 slides over from the top and fills the right wing spot 2 just left, feet set and shot-ready — 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side.
  5. 5 turns and makes his move toward the basket. The zone's bottom defender on the right block steps UP to help on him.
  6. On that step-up — not before — 4 cuts across the lane behind the defense to the now-vacated RIGHT block. 5 feeds 4 for the layup.
  7. Mirror: entry to the left wing — 5 catches on the left elbow, 4 waits on the right block, the left-block defender steps up, 4 cuts across to the left block, 3 slides to the left corner, 1 fills behind him to the left wing, and 2 stays home on the right wing — he's the skip.

Reads — what 5 does with it

  1. Right-block defender steps up to 5 → 5 feeds 4 cutting behind him to the vacated right block for the layup. Primary
  2. Right-block defender does NOT step up → 5 keeps it and scores himself — the shot or the drive is his.
  3. 5's move draws extra help → 5 kicks it to 2 spotted up in the right corner — or back out to 1, who filled in at the right wing behind him — for the three. Two kick-outs on 5's own side.
  4. Defense collapses on 4's cut at the block → 4 kicks it back out to 2 in the corner or to 1 at the wing for the three.
  5. Top of the zone collapses on 5 → skip it across to the far wing (3) — he stayed home, wide on the left — for the open three.

Coaching points

  • 4 waits opposite. He doesn't go early — he goes when the block defender steps up to 5, and he cuts behind him. Timing is the play. Go early and you drag your defender into the pass; go on the step-up and the block is empty.
  • The passer doesn't stand and watch — 2 feeds 5, then slides to the corner and is ready: feet behind the line, hands up, shot ready. He is 5's release and 4's kick-out. That slide is Base-only (and Skip): the passer slides to the corner only when it's empty — in Extend and Drag, 4 is going there, so the passer holds at the wing instead.
  • 2 goes to the corner, 1 fills behind him. 3 stays home — he's the skip. When 2 drops to the corner, the right wing is a hole — 1 slides over from the top and fills it, so 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side and the defense can't just load up on the corner and the block. 3 does not slide up to fill the top: his width on the left wing is what stretches the zone, and the moment he walks toward the top defender the skip gets easier to guard. The fill is Base-only — in Extend and Drag, 2 holds at the wing, so there's no hole and 1 stays at the top.
  • 5 must catch ready to turn — chin the ball, make a move that forces the help, then see 4's cut behind the defender, see 2 in the corner, see 1 filled in at the wing, see the skip to 3 staying home. No wasted motion.
  • Wings stay wide — and 3 stays home. The wider 2 and 3 start, the more the zone has to stretch — that's what opens the middle and makes the step-up come. 3 never chases the play toward the top; his width on the left wing is the skip.

Play 1B · Variation A — off Our Set · Call: EXTEND

Extend — Overload

The idea: Same set, same baseline run — but instead of stopping at the far block, 4 keeps going and extends ALL the way out to the three-point line in the corner — feet set behind the line, deep in the corner. Now that side is overloaded: 2 with the ball, 4 in the corner, and 5 flashing to the elbow — three attackers against the two defenders a 2-3 has on that side. And because 4 got all the way out, his defender has to follow him out — that's what gives 5 the room to sink down to the vacated low block and seal. Passer rule: because 4 takes the corner, 2 does NOT slide there — he holds at the wing, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out at the wing.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up exactly like Play 1: 4 on the low block, 5 high post, wings wide and deep.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing. (Mirror it left.)
  3. 4 runs the baseline block to block — and keeps going, extending ALL the way out to the three-point line in the far corner. Feet set behind the line, deep in the corner, ready to shoot. Do not stop short.
  4. 5 flashes to the ball-side elbow. The overload is set: 2, 4, and 5 against two defenders on that side.
  5. 2 holds at the wing — he does NOT slide to the corner. 4 is going out there (passer rule). After the swing, 2 stays spotted up at the wing behind the line, shot-ready, and becomes the kick-out there.
  6. Extend + Sink: because 4 got all the way out to the line, his defender has to follow him out — and that's what gives 5 room to sink down to the low block on that same side and seal his man. 4 feeds 5 on the block.

Reads — in this order

  1. 4 in the corner — 2 swings it down to him for the three, or 4 drives the baseline. First look
  2. 5 seals on the low block (Extend + Sink) → 2 at the wing or 4 in the corner feeds 5 for the post finish. Primary once he seals
  3. Before he sinks, 5 at the elbow can turn and shoot, or dump to the rim if the middle defender steps up.
  4. Defense doubles down on 5 → 5 kicks back out to 4 in the corner OR back out to 2 holding at the wing for the three — both are behind the line and shot-ready.
  5. Whole zone over-shifts to the overload → skip it back to 3 on the opposite wing for the open three.

Coaching points

  • 4 — get ALL the way out to the three-point line. That's what gives 5 room to sink. If 4 stops short, his defender can guard both the corner and the block, and 5 has nowhere to go. All the way out — feet behind the line, deep in the corner — and the defender must follow him, which empties the block for 5.
  • 5 goes as 4 clears — sink to the block while the defender's back is turned chasing 4 to the line. Wait, and the block is guarded again; go early, and you run into your own man's space.
  • Seal like you mean it: low, wide, target hand up — and the feed has to arrive on time, before the zone recovers.
  • Passer rule in Extend — 2 holds at the wing. If 2 slides down too, two men end up in the corner and there is no kick-out at the wing. Hold at the wing, stay behind the line, be ready: you are 5's second kick-out, after 4 in the corner.

Play 1C · Variation B — off Our Set · Call: DRAG

Drag — the Counter

The idea: Once the defense has seen Play 1, its bottom defender starts chasing 4's baseline run. Let him. 4 runs block to block and keeps going — extending ALL the way out to the three-point line in the corner, feet set behind the line, deep in the corner — dragging that defender all the way out with him. That's what gives 5 maximum space: the lane is completely empty. 5 catches at the high post and goes himself, straight down the vacated lane. Same look, different finish. Passer rule: because 4 takes the corner, 2 does NOT slide there — he holds at the wing after he feeds 5, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out at the wing.

  • Passer rule in Drag — 2 holds at the wing. Don't follow 4 to the corner. Hold behind the line at the wing and be 5's other kick-out: corner (4) or wing (2), the defense can't cover both.

Play 1D · Variation C — off Our Set · Call: SKIP

Skip — the Ball Goes Across

The idea: Same swing, same flash, same baseline run. But sometimes the zone over-shifts to the ball side — two defenders come to the ball and the far side is empty. So don't force the crowded side: skip the ball across to the far wing, whose defender left him to help. He shoots the three — and 4 is already sealed on the far block if he drives instead.

Family 2 · vs 2-3 Zone

Zone Concepts vs 2-3

Four plays, four ideas: Triangle · Kentucky · Flash · One More — continuity, the high-low roll, the flash and dump, and the overload extra pass.

What ties them together: every one attacks the same soft spots of a 2-3 — the high post, the gaps, and the man the zone leaves behind. Learn the ideas and you can call any of them against any 2-3 you see.

Zone Concepts · vs 2-3 · Play Z1

Triangle — Continuity

The idea: A continuity against the 2-3 — it never stops and never resets. The three guards work a rotating triangle at the top: pass it, fill the spot the ball just left, and keep the triangle turning. Every swing makes the top of the zone slide, and sooner or later a guard catches it with the defense a step behind — that's the shot, or the drive into the gap the rotation opened.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

How it works: there is no finish to memorize — the rotation IS the play. Pass, fill, swing it again. Run it until the zone breaks, then take what it gives you.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set the triangle: 1 top, 2 and 3 on the wings. 5 at the high post, 4 in the corner.
  2. 1 passes to 2, then fills toward the ball side. 2 rotates up to the top. The three guard spots are always filled — just by different men.
  3. Swing it across the top to 3. Every pass moves the triangle one turn.
  4. Take the first good look the rotation creates — the open three, or the drive into the gap.
  5. Nothing there? Don't reset. Keep the triangle turning and run it again.

Reads

  1. Guard catches it with his defender a step behind → shoot it. Primary
  2. Defender closes out hard → drive the gap he left, finish or kick to the corner.
  3. Zone stays solid → keep rotating. The breakdown comes on the third or fourth swing, not the first.

Coaching points

  • Pass and fill — never pass and stand. The man who just passed is the man who keeps the triangle turning.
  • Fill the spot the ball just left. If two men end up in one spot, the triangle is broken and the zone gets to rest.
  • Be patient. Continuity beats a zone with swings, not with one hero pass.

Zone Concepts · vs 2-3 · Play Z2

Kentucky — High-Low Pick & Roll

The idea: The Kentucky action. Reverse the ball to shift the whole zone one way, then enter it to the high post — the catch collapses the zone toward the middle. Now the high-low screen: 5 turns, the low man screens and rolls behind the bottom of the zone — and the zone never tags the roll man. He catches it rolling to the rim for the layup.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

The sequence: reversal first — the entry means nothing until the zone has moved. Shift them, enter it, then roll behind them.

How it runs — step by step

  • Roll man: go behind them, not through them. Stay where the bottom defender can't see you until 5 turns.
  • 5: catch ready to turn. Rim first, roll man second, kick-out third — in that order, every time.

Flash — Wing Entry, Dump Down

The idea: Simple and direct. Enter it to the wing, flash a man to the high post, and dump it down to the block. The wing entry pulls the top of the zone out, the flash catch pulls the middle up — and while everybody steps toward the ball, 4 ducks in behind them on the block and takes the dump-down to finish inside.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

The three beats: wing entry → flash catch → dump down. Every beat pulls a defender the wrong way.

Zone Concepts · vs 2-3 · Play Z4

One More — Overload & the Extra Pass

The idea: Overload one side of the zone — three attackers against the two defenders a 2-3 has over there — and make them scramble. The ball goes wing to corner, the defense flies at it, and then comes the play's name: one more pass. The extra pass beats the scramble, and the man they left to chase the ball catches it wide open.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

The rule of the play: when the defense scrambles, the next pass is the shot. Don't shoot over the closeout — move it one more time.

Family 3 · vs 1-3-1 Zone

Vs 1-3-1

One look, called on the way up: Base · Flash · One More · High-Low — the guards call one word as they bring the ball up, so everyone knows the finish before it starts.

One alignment — every call starts the same: 2-1-2 — two guards up top splitting the point defender, the middle man at the free-throw line, and two players on the baseline in the corners, behind the zone. Same set every time — only the finish changes.

Why it works: a 1-3-1 has one man at the top, one man on the baseline, and a soft middle — so we split the top man with two guards, live in the middle, and put two men behind the one baseline runner.

The idea: One base set against the 1-3-1, and this is its home look. Two guards up top split the point defender, 5 sits in the seam at the free-throw line, and two men live on the baseline behind the zone where the lone baseline defender can't see them. Swing the ball side to side until the zone shifts — then the far-side baseline runner is behind everybody for the skip pass.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up 2-1-2: two guards top splitting the point, 5 at the free-throw line, 4 and 3 in the corners behind the zone.
  2. Swing the ball side to side across the top — 1 to 2 and back. Quick, flat passes. Make the point defender pick a man.

  • Baseline runners: stay behind the zone. The moment you drift up where the baseline man can see you, the skip dies.
  • Swing it flat and fast. Slow swings let a 1-3-1 reset — fast swings make it slide until it breaks.

Play 2B · Vs 1-3-1 · Call: FLASH

Vs 1-3-1 — Flash

The idea: Same base set, same swings. This time the middle man makes the play: 5 flashes hard into the ball-side seam — the gap between the wing and the middle of the 1-3-1 — and catches it there. A catch in the seam collapses the whole defense toward him, and from there it's simple: look low first for the baseline man, then kick it back out if the wings pinch in.

Coaching points

  • "Whoever gets the rebound, find 1." Not the nearest man, not up the middle — 1, at the free throw line or the free throw line extended. That one look starts everything, every time.
  • "One dribble — pass it ahead." 1 gets one dribble, only one. The second dribble is where breaks die. One dribble, and the ball is ahead to 2 on the run.
  • "2 is always on the right sideline, 3 is always wide on the left." Always means always — no swapping sides, no drifting to the ball. The width is what stretches the defense, and 3's width is what makes the skip pass a three.
  • "4 runs the rim. 5 is the trailer." Every rep, same jobs — they don't swap. 4 sprints straight down the middle to the rim. 5 runs slow behind the play and is the last man down the floor — that's the point of the trailer: he arrives at the top of the key after the first wave, when the defense has turned its back on him.
  • 1 — pass it ahead, then go. One dribble, pass ahead to 2 — and then hustle down the floor and fill to the top of the key. Don't stay back and watch the pass. The break needs you at the top when it flows into the offense.
  • 2 — read them in order. Attack the basket first. Then 4 at the rim. Then skip it across to 3 for the three. Don't skip a read — the defense tells you which one is there.
  • "4 — if you don't get it, clear out to the ball side." If the feed at the rim isn't there, clear out toward the 2 side and get out of the middle. Standing under the rim blocks your own trailer. Clear out, and the lane is empty for 5.
  • "5 — the lane is yours, run right down the middle." Don't run straight under the basket early and disappear. Come slow behind the play, stop at the top of the key first and be a look; if nothing's there, run straight down the middle of the lane to the rim for the late pass — into the space 4 just cleared out of.
  • "No pass? We're already into our offense — no reset, no standing around." If the dive doesn't get the ball, nobody stops, nobody walks it back out. We flow straight into the half-court offense from where we are.

How it runs — the shifts

  1. Formation: two guards up top; three across the back — forwards on the blocks/wings, center in the middle of the lane.
  2. Ball to the wing: ball-side guard closes out on the wing; ball-side bottom defender steps out toward the wing/corner area; the middle man stays home protecting the lane; weak-side defenders sink toward the lane.
  3. Ball to the corner: bottom defender goes all the way out to the corner; middle man slides to the ball-side block; ball-side top guard drops to the elbow; weak side sinks in to rebounding position.
  4. Ball comes back out: everybody sprints back to formation — reset on the pass, every time.

Rules — three, that's it

  1. Protect the middle first — no layups. Make them beat us from outside.
  2. Move on the pass, not on the catch. When the ball leaves his hand, you're already moving.
  3. Every shot, five men box out and rebound. A stop isn't a stop until we have the ball.

Coaching points

  • Talk — the middle man is the quarterback. The center sees the whole floor, so he calls the ball's location all possession long: "Top!" "Wing!" "Corner!" If he's quiet, the back line is guessing — and guessing gives up layups.
  • Close out under control: sprint out, then break down with a hand up. Running past the shooter is the same as not guarding him.

Rules — three, that's it

  1. The corners are ours — trap every corner catch, every time.
  2. The baseline man never stops running — block to block with the ball.
  3. Hands up and active — we want deflections, not fouls.

Coaching points

  • The 1-3-1 is a gambling defense — trap hard, then sprint back to your spots on the pass out. A lazy trap is just two men out of position; a hard trap is a steal waiting to happen.
  • Trap with your feet and your hands up — never reach in. A reach is a foul, and a foul kills the trap.

Rules — three, that's it

  2. One pass away: in the gap. Two passes away: on the help line. Your spot depends on the ball, not just your man.
  3. Help your teammate, then sprint back to your man.

Coaching points

  • Defense moves on every pass — five men shift together like they're on a string. When the ball moves, everybody moves. If one man stands still, the string breaks and somebody is open.
  • Close out under control: sprint, then break down short with a hand up. Fly by your man and you've done his job for him.

How it runs — step by step

  1. The alignment: 5 inbounding under the basket, 1 at the top of the key, 2 just above him at the top of the arc, 3 wide on one side at the midcourt line, 4 wide on the other side at the midcourt line.
  2. 3 comes down to receive the ball from 5. 5 inbounds it to him — 3 meets the pass, he doesn't wait for it.
  3. 2 screens for 1, and 1 goes diagonally up the court off the screen. 3 looks for 1 first and hits him on the diagonal.
  4. After 2 screens for 1, 2 goes up the court down the middle. 4 goes up the other side as well — and stays wide, as a safety valve.
  5. If 1 is not open, he continues to run down the floor. 3's next look: hit 2, who is running down the middle.
  6. 5 trails the play. If there's no pass up the court, reverse it to 5.
  7. Off the reverse: 4 can come down and get the ball from 5, and 2 can go diagonal the other way to help with that.

Reads — 3 with the ball, in order

  1. 1 on the diagonal. 2's screen frees him up the court — hit him first. Read 1
  2. 2 up the middle. 1 not open? He keeps running — look middle for 2, the screener releasing up the court. Read 2
  3. Reverse to 5 trailing. No pass up the court — don't force one. 5 is behind the play and open; reverse it to him. Read 3
  4. 4, the safety valve. He stayed wide on the other side the whole way up — he's the release if the middle gets jammed, and off the reverse he comes down to get it from 5 while 2 diagonals across to help.

Coaching points

  • "3 comes to the ball." He starts at midcourt and comes all the way down to receive it — meeting the pass shortens it and takes the trap away before it starts. Nobody waits flat-footed for an inbound against a press.
  • "2 — screen him loose, then go." The screen is what frees 1 on the diagonal. Set it solid, hold it a beat — then 2 becomes the second option himself, straight up the middle. A screen-and-stand is a wasted man.
  • "1 — diagonal, not sideline." Come off the screen on the diagonal up the court. The sideline is the press's extra defender; the diagonal runs away from it. And if you're not open, keep running down the floor — clear out and let 2 be the look in the middle.
  • "3 — read them in order, don't force the first one." 1 on the diagonal first. Then 2 up the middle. A forced pass into the press is a turnover; the reads are there because one of them will be open.
  • "4 stays wide. 5 trails." 4's width is the safety valve — the moment he drifts inside, there's nowhere to release the ball. And 5 is always the reverse behind the play: if nothing up the court is there, we still have a pass we can make — back to 5 — and then 4 comes down for it and 2 helps across. There's always an out.
