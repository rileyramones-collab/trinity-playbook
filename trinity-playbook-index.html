One set for each defense — Our Set vs the 2-3, Baseline vs the 1-3-1, Five-Out vs man — and every variation runs off the same starting look.

Family 1 · vs 2-3 Zone

Our Set vs 2-3

One look, called on the way up: Base · Extend · Drag · Skip — the guard calls one word as he brings the ball up, so everyone knows the finish before it starts.

One look — every variation starts the same: swing it to a wing, 5 flashes to the elbow, 4 runs the baseline. The defense sees the same start every time — only the finish changes.

Passer rule — one rule for every call: The passer slides to the corner only when it's empty. If 4 is going out there (Extend, Drag), the passer holds at the wing and becomes the kick-out there.

Play 1 · Our Set · Call: BASE

Our Set — High-Low

The idea: This is our own set against a 2-3 — wings stretched wide, 5 in the middle of the zone, and 4 starting on the right low block. The ball swings right, 5 flashes and catches at the RIGHT elbow, and 4 waits on the LEFT block — opposite 5. Then the timing takes over: 5 turns and makes his move toward the basket, the zone's bottom defender on the RIGHT block steps UP to help on him — and on that step-up, not before, 4 cuts across the lane behind the defense to the now-vacated RIGHT block and 5 feeds him for the layup. Meanwhile 2, who fed 5, slides down to the right corner, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out — and as 2 goes, 1 slides over from the top to fill the right wing spot 2 just left, so 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side and the right-side spacing stays honest. 3 does not move — he stays home, wide on the left wing, and if the top of the zone collapses on 5 instead, he is open there for the skip-pass three.

Beats: 2-3 Zone

Call on the way up: BASE — one word from the guard, and everyone knows the finish. One look — every variation starts the same. This is Base: 4 waits opposite, then — the moment the right-block defender steps up to 5 — cuts behind him to the vacated right block for the feed. The corner is empty in Base, so the passer rule lets 2 slide down to the corner after he feeds 5 — 1 fills behind him at the right wing, and 3 stays home — he's the skip.

Mirror it — it works both ways

The whole timed action mirrors. Flip the entry to the left wing: 5 catches on the LEFT elbow → 4 waits on the RIGHT block, opposite him → the LEFT-block defender steps up to 5 → 4 cuts across behind him to the now-vacated LEFT block for the feed. And the passer mirrors too: 3 feeds 5, then slides down to the left corner as the kick-out — 1 fills behind him to the left wing, and 2 stays home, wide on the right wing — he's the skip. Same play, other direction — learn both.

Mirror finish: 3 feeds 5 at the left elbow and slides to the left corner, 1 fills behind him to the left wing, and 2 stays home on the right wing as the skip; the left-block defender steps up to 5, 4 cuts across behind him to the left block, and 5 feeds him there.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up in our set: 1 top, 2 & 3 wide and deep, 5 high post, 4 on the RIGHT low block for this rep.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing.
  3. 5 flashes hard to the RIGHT elbow. 4 runs the baseline block to block behind the zone to the LEFT block — opposite 5 — and waits there. 3 stays home, wide on the left wing — he's the skip.
  4. 2 feeds 5 at the right elbow — get the ball into the middle of the zone. Right after the feed, 2 slides down to the right corner, feet set behind the three-point line, spotted up and ready. As 2 goes, 1 slides over from the top and fills the right wing spot 2 just left, feet set and shot-ready — 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side.
  5. 5 turns and makes his move toward the basket. The zone's bottom defender on the right block steps UP to help on him.
  6. On that step-up — not before — 4 cuts across the lane behind the defense to the now-vacated RIGHT block. 5 feeds 4 for the layup.
  7. Mirror: entry to the left wing — 5 catches on the left elbow, 4 waits on the right block, the left-block defender steps up, 4 cuts across to the left block, 3 slides to the left corner, 1 fills behind him to the left wing, and 2 stays home on the right wing — he's the skip.

Reads — what 5 does with it

  1. Right-block defender steps up to 5 → 5 feeds 4 cutting behind him to the vacated right block for the layup. Primary
  2. Right-block defender does NOT step up → 5 keeps it and scores himself — the shot or the drive is his.
  3. 5's move draws extra help → 5 kicks it to 2 spotted up in the right corner — or back out to 1, who filled in at the right wing behind him — for the three. Two kick-outs on 5's own side.
  4. Defense collapses on 4's cut at the block → 4 kicks it back out to 2 in the corner or to 1 at the wing for the three.
  5. Top of the zone collapses on 5 → skip it across to the far wing (3) — he stayed home, wide on the left — for the open three.

Coaching points

  • 4 waits opposite. He doesn't go early — he goes when the block defender steps up to 5, and he cuts behind him. Timing is the play. Go early and you drag your defender into the pass; go on the step-up and the block is empty.
  • The passer doesn't stand and watch — 2 feeds 5, then slides to the corner and is ready: feet behind the line, hands up, shot ready. He is 5's release and 4's kick-out. That slide is Base-only (and Skip): the passer slides to the corner only when it's empty — in Extend and Drag, 4 is going there, so the passer holds at the wing instead.
  • 2 goes to the corner, 1 fills behind him. 3 stays home — he's the skip. When 2 drops to the corner, the right wing is a hole — 1 slides over from the top and fills it, so 5 keeps a kick-out on his own side and the defense can't just load up on the corner and the block. 3 does not slide up to fill the top: his width on the left wing is what stretches the zone, and the moment he walks toward the top defender the skip gets easier to guard. The fill is Base-only — in Extend and Drag, 2 holds at the wing, so there's no hole and 1 stays at the top.
  • 5 must catch ready to turn — chin the ball, make a move that forces the help, then see 4's cut behind the defender, see 2 in the corner, see 1 filled in at the wing, see the skip to 3 staying home. No wasted motion.
  • Wings stay wide — and 3 stays home. The wider 2 and 3 start, the more the zone has to stretch — that's what opens the middle and makes the step-up come. 3 never chases the play toward the top; his width on the left wing is the skip.

Play 1B · Variation A — off Our Set · Call: EXTEND

Extend — Overload

The idea: Same set, same baseline run — but instead of stopping at the far block, 4 keeps going and extends ALL the way out to the three-point line in the corner — feet set behind the line, deep in the corner. Now that side is overloaded: 2 with the ball, 4 in the corner, and 5 flashing to the elbow — three attackers against the two defenders a 2-3 has on that side. And because 4 got all the way out, his defender has to follow him out — that's what gives 5 the room to sink down to the vacated low block and seal. Passer rule: because 4 takes the corner, 2 does NOT slide there — he holds at the wing, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out at the wing.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up exactly like Play 1: 4 on the low block, 5 high post, wings wide and deep.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing. (Mirror it left.)
  3. 4 runs the baseline block to block — and keeps going, extending ALL the way out to the three-point line in the far corner. Feet set behind the line, deep in the corner, ready to shoot. Do not stop short.
  4. 5 flashes to the ball-side elbow. The overload is set: 2, 4, and 5 against two defenders on that side.
  5. 2 holds at the wing — he does NOT slide to the corner. 4 is going out there (passer rule). After the swing, 2 stays spotted up at the wing behind the line, shot-ready, and becomes the kick-out there.
  6. Extend + Sink: because 4 got all the way out to the line, his defender has to follow him out — and that's what gives 5 room to sink down to the low block on that same side and seal his man. 4 feeds 5 on the block.

Reads — in this order

  1. 4 in the corner — 2 swings it down to him for the three, or 4 drives the baseline. First look
  2. 5 seals on the low block (Extend + Sink) → 2 at the wing or 4 in the corner feeds 5 for the post finish. Primary once he seals
  3. Before he sinks, 5 at the elbow can turn and shoot, or dump to the rim if the middle defender steps up.
  4. Defense doubles down on 5 → 5 kicks back out to 4 in the corner OR back out to 2 holding at the wing for the three — both are behind the line and shot-ready.
  5. Whole zone over-shifts to the overload → skip it back to 3 on the opposite wing for the open three.

Coaching points

  • 4 — get ALL the way out to the three-point line. That's what gives 5 room to sink. If 4 stops short, his defender can guard both the corner and the block, and 5 has nowhere to go. All the way out — feet behind the line, deep in the corner — and the defender must follow him, which empties the block for 5.
  • 5 goes as 4 clears — sink to the block while the defender's back is turned chasing 4 to the line. Wait, and the block is guarded again; go early, and you run into your own man's space.
  • Seal like you mean it: low, wide, target hand up — and the feed has to arrive on time, before the zone recovers.
  • Passer rule in Extend — 2 holds at the wing. If 2 slides down too, two men end up in the corner and there is no kick-out at the wing. Hold at the wing, stay behind the line, be ready: you are 5's second kick-out, after 4 in the corner.

Play 1C · Variation B — off Our Set · Call: DRAG

Drag — the Counter

The idea: Once the defense has seen Play 1, its bottom defender starts chasing 4's baseline run. Let him. 4 runs block to block and keeps going — extending ALL the way out to the three-point line in the corner, feet set behind the line, deep in the corner — dragging that defender all the way out with him. That's what gives 5 maximum space: the lane is completely empty. 5 catches at the high post and goes himself, straight down the vacated lane. Same look, different finish. Passer rule: because 4 takes the corner, 2 does NOT slide there — he holds at the wing after he feeds 5, spotted up behind the line as the kick-out at the wing.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up exactly like Play 1: 4 on the low block, 5 high post, wings wide and deep.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing. (Mirror it left.)
  3. 4 runs the baseline block to block — and keeps going, extending ALL the way out to the three-point line in the far corner. Feet set behind the line, deep in the corner, ready to shoot. Do not stop in the short corner. That full extension drags the chasing bottom defender all the way out of the lane with him.
  4. 5 flashes to the high post / elbow and 2 feeds him — then 2 holds at the wing. He does NOT slide to the corner: 4 is out there (passer rule). 2 stays spotted up behind the line at the wing and becomes the kick-out there.
  5. 5 turns and dives down the middle — straight down the lane 4's defender just vacated, with all the space 4's extension created — and finishes himself.

Reads — in this order

  1. Lane is open → 5 drives it himself for the layup. Primary
  2. Defense collapses on 5's dive → kick out to 4 spotted up at the three-point line in the corner for the three OR back out to 2 holding at the wing — both behind the line and shot-ready — or across to 3.
  3. The bottom defender refuses to chase 4 all the way out → 4 is alone at the line: swing or skip it to him in the corner for the three.

Coaching points

  • 4 — get ALL the way out to the three-point line. That's what gives 5 room on the dive. If 4 stops in the short corner, his defender can still recover into the lane and help on 5. All the way out — feet behind the line, deep in the corner — and the defender must follow him out there, which empties the lane completely for 5.
  • Same look, different finish — the run doesn't just clear space, it drags the one defender who could protect the rim all the way out to the line. Sell Play 1 first; Drag only works because they fear it.
  • 5: catch, turn, go. One hard dribble at most — the lane won't stay open, so don't dance with it.
  • 4 stays shot-ready at the line — if the defense collapses on 5's dive, the kick-out to 4 in the corner is the reward.
  • Passer rule in Drag — 2 holds at the wing. Don't follow 4 to the corner. Hold behind the line at the wing and be 5's other kick-out: corner (4) or wing (2), the defense can't cover both.

Play 1D · Variation C — off Our Set · Call: SKIP

Skip — the Ball Goes Across

The idea: Same swing, same flash, same baseline run. But sometimes the zone over-shifts to the ball side — two defenders come to the ball and the far side is empty. So don't force the crowded side: skip the ball across to the far wing, whose defender left him to help. He shoots the three — and 4 is already sealed on the far block if he drives instead.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up exactly like Play 1: 4 on the right low block, 5 high post, wings wide and deep.
  2. 1 passes to 2. 5 flashes to the right elbow and 4 runs the baseline to the left block — opposite 5, same as Base.
  3. Read the zone: if it over-shifts to the ball and 3's defender leaves him to help, the crowded side is closed.

Reads — in this order

  1. Far wing is open → 3 catches and shoots the three. Primary
  2. Closeout comes hard → 3 drives it — one dribble past him, finish or dump to 4 sealed on the far block.

Coaching points

  • If two defenders come to the ball, the ball goes across. Don't force the crowded side — the open man is the one they left.
  • The far wing stays ready — feet set, hands up, shot-ready the whole time the ball is on the other side. The skip only works if he can catch and shoot in one motion.
  • Throw the skip with zip, over the top — a lazy skip gets stolen. Two hands, on a line.
  • 4: seal and stay on the far block — you're the driver's dump-off and the rebounder if the three misses.

Baseline

The idea: A 1-3-1 only has one man on the baseline — so we put two men down there. Swing the ball, send 5 running the baseline behind the zone, then skip it to the corner. One defender can't guard both.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up 2-1-2: two guards top, 3 right wing, 4 left corner, 5 right block.
  2. Swing the ball 1 → 2 → 3 to the right wing. Quick passes — make the zone move.
  3. As the ball moves, 5 runs the baseline behind the defense to the left block. 4 stays in the left corner.
  4. 3 skip-passes over the top to 4 in the corner.
  5. Read the baseline defender and finish (see Reads).

Reads — what 4 does with it

  1. Baseline man runs out at 4 → 4 dumps it inside to 5 for the layup. Primary
  2. Baseline man stays with 5 → 4 shoots the corner three.
  3. Wing defender drops down to help → kick it back out and swing it again. Patience wins.

Coaching points

  • The corners and the baseline are the 1-3-1's weak spots — that's where we live against this defense.
  • Be patient and swing the ball until the defense shifts — the skip only works after the zone moves.
  • The baseline runner stays behind the zone where the bottom defender loses sight of him.

Family 3 · vs Man-to-Man

Five-Out vs Man — Pass & Screen Away

One look, called on the way up: Base · Fade · Slip · Swap · Horns — the guard calls one word as he brings the ball up, and the finish matches the call.

One look — every variation starts the same: all five outside the three-point line — 1 at the top, 2 on the right wing, 3 on the left wing, 4 in the right corner, 5 in the left corner. Full five-out spacing, nobody in the lane.

The one rule — pass it, then screen away: Whoever passes goes away from the ball and screens for the next man. The cutter reads his defender, the screener reads his — and if nothing is there, reverse it through the top and run it again on the other side. The offense flows; it never resets.

Play 3 · Five-Out · Call: BASE

Five-Out — Pass & Screen Away

The idea: Five out, one rule: pass it, then screen away. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing, then goes away and sets a screen for 3 on the left wing. 3 curls off that screen down the middle to the rim — the first look — and 2 feeds him for the layup. 4 and 5 hold their spacing in the corners and stay shot-ready, so their defenders can't help. If 3 isn't open, the ball reverses back through the top and the same action runs on the other side — no reset, no standing around.

How it runs — step by step

  1. Set up five out: 1 top, 2 right wing, 3 left wing, 4 right corner, 5 left corner — all behind the line.
  2. 1 passes to 2 on the right wing.
  3. 1 goes away — opposite the pass — and sets a screen for 3 on the left wing. He does not follow his pass.
  4. 3 waits for the screen, then curls off it down the middle to the rim, shoulder-to-shoulder with 1.
  5. 2 feeds 3 on the curl for the layup. First look
  6. 4 and 5 hold the corners, shot-ready — their spacing is what keeps the lane empty.
  7. 3 not open? 2 reverses it back through 1 at the top, and the same action runs the other way — pass and screen away, again.

Reads — what decides it

  1. 3's man trails behind the screen → 3 curls down the middle and 2 feeds him. Primary
  2. 3's man overplays the middle → that's Fade — 3 goes the other way (next play).
  3. The defense jumps the curl with the screener's man → that's Slip — 1 goes himself (Play 3C).
  4. Nothing is there → reverse it through the top and run it again. Never force the first look.

Coaching points

  • Pass and screen AWAY — every time. The passer never follows his pass and never stands. Pass it, go screen for the next man. That's the whole offense.
  • 3: wait for the screen. Leave early and your man beats you to the middle; come off it shoulder-to-shoulder and he's trailing the whole way down the lane.
  • Corners: hold your spacing. 4 and 5 drifting in is what lets defenders help on the curl. Stay wide, stay behind the line, stay shot-ready.
  • If it isn't there, it isn't there — reverse it and the same action comes right back on the other side. The flow is the play.

Play 3B · Variation A — off Five-Out · Call: FADE

Fade — Middle's Taken, Go the Other Way

The idea: Same pass, same screen away — but 3's defender overplays him to take away the middle curl. So 3 fades back off the screen to the left wing, behind the line instead. 1 finishes his screen and pops back to the top as the reversal outlet: 2 passes back to 1, and 1 swings it to 3 on the left wing for the three. The screen works both ways — middle's taken, go the other way.

  4. 4, the safety valve. He stayed wide on the other side the whole way up — he's the release if the middle gets jammed, and off the reverse he comes down to get it from 5 while 2 diagonals across to help.

Coaching points

  • "3 comes to the ball." He starts at midcourt and comes all the way down to receive it — meeting the pass shortens it and takes the trap away before it starts. Nobody waits flat-footed for an inbound against a press.
  • "2 — screen him loose, then go." The screen is what frees 1 on the diagonal. Set it solid, hold it a beat — then 2 becomes the second option himself, straight up the middle. A screen-and-stand is a wasted man.
  • "1 — diagonal, not sideline." Come off the screen on the diagonal up the court. The sideline is the press's extra defender; the diagonal runs away from it. And if you're not open, keep running down the floor — clear out and let 2 be the look in the middle.
  • "3 — read them in order, don't force the first one." 1 on the diagonal first. Then 2 up the middle. A forced pass into the press is a turnover; the reads are there because one of them will be open.
  • "4 stays wide. 5 trails." 4's width is the safety valve — the moment he drifts inside, there's nowhere to release the ball. And 5 is always the reverse behind the play: if nothing up the court is there, we still have a pass we can make — back to 5 — and then 4 comes down for it and 2 helps across. There's always an out.
